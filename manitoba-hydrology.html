Background

Why does it matter?

Rivers in Manitoba play a central role in water supply, ecological stability, agriculture, and flood management. The province faces increasing pressure from climate variability, agricultural expansion, and changing groundwater recharge patterns.

Understanding the relationship between total streamflow and the slower, groundwater-sustained baseflow component is key to assessing long-term regional water security and ecological resilience.

Streamflow

The total amount of water moving through a river at any given time — including surface runoff, interflow, and groundwater contributions.

Baseflow

The sustained groundwater-fed portion of streamflow that maintains river levels during dry periods, reflecting aquifer connectivity.

Baseflow Index

BFI =  baseflow streamflow

Higher BFI → stable, groundwater-supported system

Research Questions

What we set out to answer

01

What are the main patterns and relationships between streamflow and baseflow across different hydrological stations in Manitoba?

02

How do variations in these flow characteristics reflect differences in watershed behavior and flow regime stability across Manitoba?

Hydrological indicators were then engineered from the raw series: BFI, Flashiness (Richards-Baker index), Flow Quantiles (Q10, Q50, Q90), and a Seasonality Index — forming the feature matrix for PCA and clustering.

Methodology

Four analytical lenses

PCA

Principal Component Analysis

Condensed six correlated hydrologic variables into independent components. PC1 captures flow magnitude (Q10/Q50/Q90); PC2 captures variability vs. stability (Flashiness + Seasonality). Together they explain ~81% of total variance.

K⟨⟩

K-Means Clustering

Grouped the 10 stations into three interpretable hydrologic types based on PCA scores — revealing spatial gradients from high-BFI Shield rivers in the north to flashier prairie clay-basin systems in the south.

MLP

MLP Neural Network

Feed-forward network using the previous 7 days of streamflow to predict next-day flow. Achieves RMSE of 0.15–0.55 units depending on station, capturing short-term trends and recession limbs.

LSTM

Long Short-Term Memory

Sequential model with memory cells, receiving a 7-day input window. Outperforms MLP by capturing recession curves, seasonal cycles, and long-range temporal dependencies inherent in hydrologic time series.

Three Hydrologic Clusters

K-means identified three distinct station groups that map cleanly onto interpretable landscape types across Manitoba.

Cluster 0 (Blue) — Moderate-to-high BFI, stable flow, moderate discharge. Groundwater-connected systems.

Cluster 1 (Orange) — High-discharge stations with strong seasonal signals and lower variability index.

Cluster 2 (Green) — Lower BFI, flashier flow regimes, more event-driven variability.

Spatial Interpretation

Northern stations (e.g., 05RB003 Bloodvein River, BFI = 0.995) cluster near permeable Shield bedrock with high groundwater recharge. Southern prairie stations (e.g., 05OF003 Boyne River, BFI = 0.577) sit in clay-rich basins dominated by surface runoff — two ends of the same hydrologic spectrum.

LSTMs provided consistently stronger predictions by leveraging temporal memory across the 7-day window. Conclusion

Summary of findings

Streamflow–Baseflow Patterns

  • BFI values ranged widely from 0.577 (Boyne River) to 0.995 (Bloodvein River), reflecting major differences in groundwater connectivity across Manitoba's landscape.
  • High-BFI stations maintain stable, sustained flows year-round, while low-BFI stations display flashier, precipitation-driven dynamics.
  • PCA revealed that flow magnitude and flow variability operate as two largely independent axes of variation.

Watershed Behavior & Predictability

  • K-means clustering confirmed three ecologically meaningful hydrologic types aligned with Manitoba's geology — from permeable Shield bedrock to prairie clay basins.
  • LSTM outperformed MLP across all stations, validating the importance of temporal memory in hydrologic forecasting.
  • Stable, groundwater-supported rivers are more predictable; flashier prairie systems demand models that handle abrupt non-linearities.
